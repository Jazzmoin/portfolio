## Wallflowers

### Context

I have a collection of LEGO flowers gifted to me by my loved ones that used to sit tucked away on my shelf. For a while, I struggled to find a place where they could be properly seen and appreciated. My desk needed to remain clear for my projects, and my existing shelving was too high and enclosed. I then thought: what if I mounted them to the wall like a frame?

<img src={originalStorage} alt="Original flower storage setup" />

### The Brief

The brief I set for myself was quite simple: create a colourful vase that could be easily mounted to the wall and bring a little more colour into my room.

### The Problem

The biggest challenge was figuring out how to mount the vase securely. The flowers were surprisingly heavy, so I needed a mechanism that was rigid enough to keep everything level, even with the weight unevenly distributed.
I started looking around the house for inspiration, taking note of how things like picture frames, floating shelves and lamps were mounted. Then I spotted the battery charger for my dad's power tools, which slots neatly onto screws fixed to the wall. That seemed like a pretty good starting point, so I decided to work from there.

<img src={vaseSketches} alt="Vase drafting in pen" />

<img src={vasePaint} alt="Painting design onto vase" />

### Insights

I figured 3D printing would be the best approach, giving me the freedom to shape the vases however I liked. But the bare plastic looked a little too plain (and a bit too much like a toy), so I decided to experiment with some wall paint to give the surface more texture. I then hand-painted the designs onto each vase to give them a bit more personality.

### The Solution

I'm pretty proud of how this project turned out. It's the first self-guided design project I've worked on that solves a problem in my life so well. What started as a way to get my LEGO flowers off the shelf has turned into a little display that gives them the attention they deserve, while keeping my desk free for other projects.
