## Wallflower

### Context

I have a collection of LEGO flowers gifted to me by my loved ones that used to sit tucked away on my shelf. For a while, I struggled to find a place where they could be properly seen and appreciated. I needed my desk to remain clear for work, and my existing shelving was too high and enclosed for them to be seen. I then thought: what if I mounted them to the wall like a picture frame?

<img src={originalStorage} alt="Original flower storage setup" />

### The Brief

The brief I set for myself was quite simple: create a colourful vase that could be easily mounted to the wall and bring a little more colour into my room.

### The Problem

The biggest challenge was figuring out how to mount the vase securely. The flowers were surprisingly heavy, so I needed a mechanism that was rigid enough to keep everything level. I couldn't count on weight being evenly distributed for it to be straight, I learnt that the hard way with my first prototype.
I started looking around the house for inspiration, taking note of how things like picture frames, floating shelves and lamps were mounted. Then I spotted the battery charger for my dad's power tools, which slots neatly onto screws fixed to the wall. That seemed like a pretty good starting point, so I decided to work from there.

<img src={vaseSketches} alt="Vase drafting in pen" />

<img src={vasePaint} alt="Painting design onto vase" />

### Insights

I figured 3D printing would be the best approach, giving me the freedom to shape the vase however I liked. However, the bare plastic looked a little too plain (and a bit too much like a toy), so I decided to experiment with some wall paint to give the surface more texture. I then hand-painted the designs onto each vase to give them a bit more personality and life.

### The Solution

Once I had given the vases a protective clear coat, I hung them up and organised my flowers into cute little bouquets (ten flowers turned out to be too much for one vase, so now I have two!). I'm pretty proud of how this project turned out. It's the first self-guided design project I've worked on that solves a problem in my life so well. What started as a way to get my LEGO flowers off my shelf has turned into a piece of functional art that gives them the attention they deserve, while keeping my desk free for other projects.
